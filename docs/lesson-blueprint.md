# Lesson blueprint for Kampung Kucing Misi Jejak Sejarah

This blueprint describes a functional prototype for Form 2 Sejarah revision. It is a lesson design, not a claim that the lesson has been conducted or that student engagement has improved. The classroom incident for the assignment must come from a real observation or teacher interview. The lecturer should confirm whether a custom-developed game is acceptable as the selected tool in Question 1; the brief does not explicitly settle this.

## Lesson overview

- Subject and level: Sejarah, Tingkatan 2, secondary school.
- Topic: Chapter 2.2, Kegiatan Ekonomi Masyarakat Kerajaan Alam Melayu.
- Curriculum alignment: SP 5.2.2, describing the economic activities of societies in the Alam Melayu kingdoms.
- Duration: 40 minutes.
- Proposed context: mixed-attainment Form 2 learners revising a previously taught topic. School type, class size, device availability and the real incident still need to be supplied by the student author.
- Tool selected for the proposed lesson: Kampung Kucing Misi Jejak Sejarah, compared with Kahoot! in Question 1.
- Strategy: self-paced exploration and mastery missions with private formative feedback, hints, retries and four completion fish. There is no countdown, speed bonus, elimination or public ranking.
- Public prototype: https://miruladzim.github.io/kampung-kucing/
- Direct activity mode without 3D controls: https://miruladzim.github.io/kampung-kucing/?mode=ringan

## Intended classroom problem

The design addresses low participation and passive task completion during Sejarah revision. The working explanation is that repetitive questioning and worksheets may offer limited choice, contextual visual support and immediate feedback. This is a design hypothesis; do not describe invented classroom behaviours or a fictional teacher interview as observed evidence.

## Measurable objectives

By the end of the lesson, learners should be able to:

1. Name the four groups of economic activities: agriculture; trade; collection of forest and marine products; and mining and manufacturing. Assess this in the written exit reflection and oral discussion.
2. Correctly classify at least eight of the twelve individual examples across the three sorting activities on the first submission. The report provides item-level first-attempt totals as well as whole-question correctness.
3. Explain two factors supporting economic activities, using examples from the mission materials. The teacher evaluates this explanation in the exit reflection; the prototype does not automatically grade written reasoning.

## Prototype structure

1. Pertanian: irrigation function; sorting crops and collected goods; selecting actions relevant to interrupted irrigation.
2. Perdagangan: interpreting a schematic trading meeting point; selecting harbour facilities; connecting agricultural surplus to trade.
3. Hasil Hutan dan Laut: sorting sources; matching rattan to its use; distinguishing collecting goods from trading them.
4. Perlombongan dan Pembuatan: sorting activities; explaining transformation into goods; recognising links among economic activities.

The twelve assessment concepts are implemented as six physical pickup/delivery tasks, two pairs of facility operations checked at a gong, one two-delivery economic chain, and three physical sorting tasks with four examples each. Records still map to six single-answer, three two-answer and three sorting assessments. The main play button enters the village directly, and F acts on nearby objects; no quiz dialog appears in this flow. The book is a progress and direction journal. The 3D village is a fictional learning hub. The diagrams are original conceptual illustrations, not reconstructions of a named ancient kingdom, a real archaeological site, or an actual historical route.

## Feedback and assessment data

Wrong answers receive a targeted conceptual prompt and another opportunity. Correct answers receive an explanation. Hints are recorded separately, including whether one was used before the first answer. Completed activities can be reviewed without changing their original attempt records. A station earns exactly one fish after all three tasks have been solved.

CSV contains one row for each question, including unanswered questions. Fields include alias, station, concept, first and latest answers, first-attempt correctness, hint-before-first-response, number of attempts, sorting-item totals, follow-up guidance, timestamp and the learner's exit reflection. Data is stored in the current browser. There is no login, central teacher dashboard, automatic sharing or cross-device synchronisation. On a shared device, export the completed report before selecting Sesi baharu.

First attempts are made after the learner has access to the revision visuals and notes. They are formative revision evidence, not a pre-test of unaided prior knowledge. A hint-supported answer is not reported as an independent correct first answer.

## Learning theory connection

The cognitive theory of multimedia learning informs the use of relevant diagrams alongside concise verbal prompts (Mayer, 2009). Irrigation arrows and a schematic harbour support the specific question being answered. Short prompts accompany the physical tasks; delivering water visibly irrigates the rice plot. The village remains active during tasks. Only the progress journal, guide and report pause movement. Optional narration belongs to the alternative non-3D activity mode. This is a theory-informed design rationale, not proof that this prototype improves learning. Peer explanation and teacher questioning remain necessary to examine the reasoning behind clicks.

## Development reflection and evidence

The first educational iteration opened quiz cards from a mission book. User review found that this felt disconnected from the 3D game. The revision moved the assessment actions into the world: carrying resources, choosing delivery destinations, physically sorting goods and operating facilities. The book now tracks progress and directions. The renderer and assessment logic remain separate so an optional non-3D alternative can assess the same concepts. This alternative has separate progress records, so questionnaire results are not presented as completed physical gameplay. Retries retain the first checked attempt and assisted status; a completion reward does not replace diagnostic evidence.

## Rationale and practical limits

The missions connect a broad topic to visible tasks, offer learner choice, and provide response-specific feedback that a worksheet checked after class cannot provide immediately. However, a visually attractive village alone is not evidence of learning or reduced boredom. The teacher must inspect responses, ask for explanations and adapt instruction. Test the prototype on school devices and use direct activity mode when 3D performance or navigation is a barrier. Speech output depends on browser support and available voices; diagrams and complete text remain accessible without audio.

## Prototype verification

The automated test suite checks twelve assessment questions, first-attempt preservation, hint tracking, sorting-item totals, idempotent rewards, saved-state restoration and CSV escaping, alongside village-life, cat-motion and pond tests. Before the in-world revision, a browser walkthrough completed all twelve quiz activities using the visible controls and deliberately answered one first question incorrectly. The resulting report retained 11 correct first attempts, one retry, twelve completed activities and four fish. The actual browser-downloaded CSV was parsed and checked for twelve records, the preserved error and the exit reflection. That earlier version shared records with non-3D mode. The revised in-world version intentionally uses a separate storage key. These are prototype QA checks, not an evaluation with learners.


## In-world revision — 19 October 2026

The playable village now hosts the assessment actions itself. Four work areas sit along the central road. The cat carries a visible object at its mouth; F and the same touch button pick up, place, operate or check a nearby object. The small mission panel contains instructions and hints, not answer buttons. Sorting requires all four items to be placed before the gong records an attempt. Incorrect arrangements can be picked up and corrected; operations can be toggled before checking. The final chain records its two deliveries together, preserving retries and the first checked chain.

Water appears and rice grows when irrigation is restored; the interrupted-irrigation task temporarily dries the plot until the two relevant components are handled. Cargo, an anyaman basket and pottery appear as related tasks are completed. Completion records survive reload; unchecked arrangements restart. The book and report remain optional overlays; the 3D learning tasks do not navigate to another page.

Automated checks exercise all twelve physical mission paths, incorrect deliveries, an incomplete sorting check, corrections, hints, first-attempt records, two-part operations, the two-delivery chain, one-time rewards and reload persistence. These are software QA, not student learning results. The 40-minute timing remains a proposed plan and needs a classroom pilot, especially because physical navigation and repeated carrying take time.

Browser QA for the revision confirmed direct entry to 3D gameplay, water pickup and irrigation delivery, visual rice-plot restoration, the next sorting task appearing without navigation, rotan pickup and physical basket placement using the touch action, progress restoration after reload, the journal returning to gameplay, and the report retaining the one checked task while an unchecked arrangement remains unassessed. No browser warnings or errors were captured. Images 05 and 06 in the evidence folder record this revision; use 01, 02, 05 and 06 as a four-stage development sequence. Automated scene tests additionally complete all twelve interactions; they do not substitute for classroom evaluation.

The 3D CSV is marked `world-v2` and records actual delivery destinations and operations, rather than substituting the labels from the alternative quiz. In the final physical chain, order matters: mineral material goes to the workshop before the finished pot goes to the trader. Reversed deliveries remain an incorrect first attempt after reload.
